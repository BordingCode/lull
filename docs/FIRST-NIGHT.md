# First night with the baby monitor

This checklist takes ten minutes. Do it **on an evening when it doesn't matter
if something misbehaves** — not the first time you really need it.

## Before bed

### On both phones
1. Open **https://bordingcode.github.io/lull/** in Chrome.
2. Menu (⋮) → **Add to Home screen**. Do this. As an installed app it may keep
   playing sound with the screen off — a normal browser tab doesn't get the same.
3. Open Lull from the home-screen icon afterwards, not from the browser.

### On the phone by the baby
4. **Night** tab → Baby monitor → **This phone is with the baby**. Write the code
   down, or tap **Send link**.
5. Tap **Start monitoring** and allow the microphone.
6. **Plug it in.** It keeps its screen on all night.
7. Turn the brightness right down. The screen goes black by itself after a few
   seconds — only a small green dot is left.
8. Put it where it can hear the baby but can't be reached from the cot. Not in
   the cot, not under a blanket: it gets warm from being on.

### On your own phone
9. **Night** tab → **This phone is with me**, type the code, tap **Connect**.
   (Or open the link from step 4.)
10. It should say **Listening** in green.
11. **Tap the screen once.** The browser only allows sound after a tap. If a
    yellow warning says so, the alarm is silent — tap.
12. ⚙️ → **Allow notifications** (an extra channel on top of the sound).
13. ⚙️ → **Test the alarm**. Turn the screen off straight away and listen that
    it really wails through a dark screen. **This is the most important test.**
14. Tap **‹ Lull** to use the rest of the app. The **🎧 Listening** chip at the
    top stays, and the monitor jumps back by itself on a noise or a lost link.

## Set the sensitivity
Stand by the baby and watch the big bar on your phone.

- When it's quiet the bar is low. The **yellow line** is the alarm level.
- Talk normally by the cot and see how high the bar goes.
- Set **Sensitivity** so the yellow line sits just above the quiet background
  but below the level the baby makes.
- False alarms: turn sensitivity down, or raise the time in ⚙️ so a sound must
  last longer before the alarm goes.
- Playing a sleep sound on the baby phone? The microphone hears it too — set the
  line above it.

## If the baby phone can't be charged (pram)
The screen must stay on or Android switches the microphone off, so without a
charger the battery decides how long the monitor lasts.

- The app notices it isn't charging and **turns itself down**: the dot stops
  breathing and it sends less often.
- The baby phone shows **how many percent it uses per hour and how long is
  left** — measured on that phone, not guessed. Your phone shows it too.
- You're told at **20%**, again at **10%**, and if it drains so fast that less
  than half an hour is left.
- **Turn auto-brightness off and the brightness right down.** In sunlight the
  phone turns itself up to full and the battery goes much faster.
- Turn on the baby phone's own battery saver.
- Away from home Wi-Fi the TURN relay should be on (see the project notes),
  otherwise the two phones can't always find each other on mobile data.

Rule of thumb: **a pram nap is fine. A whole night without power is not.**

## Try the three failures before you trust it

| Do this on the baby phone | Your phone should | How fast |
|---|---|---|
| Press the power button | wail: "went into the background" | straight away |
| Turn on airplane mode | wail: "no contact" | about 8 seconds |
| Turn airplane mode off again | find its way back to **Listening** | 5–20 seconds |

The lost-connection alarm **does not stop by itself**. On purpose. Tap **OK**.

## In the morning
- Check it still says **Listening** and the baby phone is still on.
- Look at your own phone's battery. Expect a few percent per hour. Much more
  means something is wrong.

## If something goes wrong
- **"Waiting for the other phone…"** — the other one isn't open, or the codes
  differ. Check the code on both.
- **No sound, but it says Listening** — turn up Volume, and check "Quiet until
  the baby makes a sound" is off.
- **Can't connect when you're not on the same Wi-Fi** — the TURN relay is
  missing.
- **Everything is odd** — ⚙️ → Forget pairing on both phones, and pair again.

## Remember what this is
A home-made helper, not a medical device, and not an alarm system tested by
anyone but us. Use it as an extra ear.
